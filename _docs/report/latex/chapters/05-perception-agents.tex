\subsection{General Architecture}
\label{subsec:perception-arch}
\rmq{The design shared by all four modules, explained once here so the module sections need
only report what differs: grouped cross-validation by case, calibrated confidence fitted
out-of-fold, an explicit declaration of scope, and a common output format. State the
reporting convention again in one sentence --- built, measured, concluded --- since this is
the chapter where it does the most work.}

% =========================================================================================
\subsection{Triage Agent}
\label{subsec:triage-agent}

\subsubsection{Objective}
\rmq{Estimate an urgency tier from structured patient data alone, calibrated well enough
that its confidence is comparable with an imaging finding's. State what the agent is NOT
for: it does not diagnose, and it never sees an image.}

\subsubsection{Dataset}
\rmq{The pooled emergency dataset: sources, visit counts, the tier definition (low / medium
/ high) and why three tiers rather than the five-level scales used in hospital triage --- the
reasoning layer consumes the tier to decide escalation, not to assign a waiting time. Refer
back to Chapter~\ref{sec:data} for provenance rather than repeating it.}

\subsubsection{Preprocessing}
\rmq{Sentinel values are the point here. Vitals recorded as 0 mean "not measured", not a
physiological zero, and were converted to genuine missing values so the model treats them as
absent rather than as an extreme reading. Note that gradient-boosted trees handle missing
values natively, which is part of why the family was chosen.}

\subsubsection{Feature Engineering}
\rmq{Two things to report, in this order.

First, the clinically motivated composites --- shock index, a partial qSOFA, and the
red-flag thresholds --- with the reasoning that these encode how a clinician actually reads
a set of vitals, rather than leaving the model to rediscover it.

Second, and more valuable, the features that were REMOVED and why. Disposition-related
fields produced a markedly higher accuracy that reflected the outcome leaking into the
input rather than clinical signal. Give both numbers, the inflated one and the honest one.
Also report the coarse reason-for-visit grouping that was tested and rejected because it
lost accuracy: collapsing hundreds of codes into a few categories discards real signal.}

\subsubsection{Model Selection}
\rmq{The comparison against logistic regression, random forest, a small MLP and CatBoost on
identical data, and the result that none beat gradient-boosted trees. Make the argument
explicitly: this establishes that the accuracy ceiling is a property of the data, not a
limitation of the architecture, which is what licenses the interpretation in
Section~\ref{subsec:triage-error}.}

\subsubsection{Training}
\rmq{The temporal split (train on the earlier year, test on the later), the class weighting,
and the additional weight on the most urgent classes. Report that a more aggressive weight
was tested and rejected because it cost substantial accuracy for negligible safety gain ---
the sweet spot was found by measurement, not by assumption.}

\subsubsection{Results}
\label{subsec:triage-results}
\rmq{Accuracy and macro F1 against the majority-class baseline, the confusion matrix, and
per-source performance. A single pooled figure hides whether the model transfers between
sources, so report the breakdown.

Include the context that makes the number interpretable: published inter-rater agreement
between human triage nurses on the same scale. A model that matches the agreement level of
trained clinicians is in a different position from one that simply scores low, and the
reader cannot know which without that reference.}

\subsubsection{Error Analysis}
\label{subsec:triage-error}
\rmq{Not all errors cost the same. Report the dangerous-error rate --- high-urgency cases
predicted low --- separately from overall error, and introduce accuracy-within-one-level as
the metric appropriate to an ordinal target. Then the safety threshold: a "low" prediction
is forbidden when the probability of "high" exceeds a fixed bound. Report the threshold
sweep and what it bought: roughly half the dangerous errors for almost no overall accuracy.}

\subsubsection{Calibration}
\rmq{Method, the data it was fitted on (a held-out slice of training, never the test set),
and ECE before and after. State plainly that accuracy is essentially unchanged: calibration
does not make a model more accurate, it makes its stated confidence mean something.}

\subsubsection{Final Decision}
\rmq{The configuration retained, and the limitations declared to the reasoning layer.}

\subsection{Lung Module}
\label{subsec:lung-module}

\subsubsection{Objective}
\rmq{Detect four findings --- B-lines, consolidation, pleural effusion, pleural thickening
--- from a lung ultrasound clip. Emphasise that this is deliberately not a disease
classifier: it reports findings, and the inference to pneumonia or oedema belongs to the
reasoning layer.}

\subsubsection{Dataset and the Multi-Label Formulation}
\rmq{Why multi-label rather than single-label: a clip can show B-lines and consolidation at
once, so the model emits four independent probabilities rather than one distribution. This
is the first structural difference from the cardiac module and worth stating explicitly.}

\subsubsection{Preprocessing and Clip Handling}
\rmq{Eight evenly spaced frames per clip rather than every frame, since neighbouring frames
are near-duplicates; frames decoded once and cached; per-frame inference aggregated to a
clip probability by the median, so one blurred frame cannot dominate. Justify the median
choice: frames from a clip are not independent evidence.}

\subsubsection{Partially Labelled External Data}
\rmq{The extra images positive for B-lines only, with no negatives and no labels for the
other three findings. Report the solution --- a per-sample mask restricting the loss to the
column that is actually known --- and the discipline around it: these samples entered the
training set only, never validation.}

\subsubsection{Model Selection and the Frozen-Backbone Discovery}
\rmq{The single most valuable diagnostic in the project. An earlier configuration had the
entire backbone frozen, leaving only the classifier head trainable, and since ultrasound
speckle resembles nothing in ImageNet the frozen features could not adapt. Report the
parameter counts before and after, and the measured effect of unfreezing the last blocks
with a low learning rate --- the largest single gain recorded in this project.}

\subsubsection{Training}
\rmq{Mixed precision, the cosine learning-rate schedule, early stopping on validation AUROC,
and the epoch budget. Keep it brief; the interesting decisions are elsewhere.}

\subsubsection{Results}
\label{subsec:lung-results}
\rmq{Per-finding and macro AUROC at CASE level, with the clip and case counts behind each
figure. Report the before/after of the leakage fix explicitly: the earlier, higher numbers
were inflated by patient overlap between folds, and the honest figures are lower. Give both.
State which findings have too few positive cases to support a reliable threshold; those are
marked unreliable and the mark is carried through to the reasoning layer.}

\subsubsection{Error Analysis}
\rmq{Which findings the model confuses and whether the confusions are clinically adjacent.
Relate the weakest findings to their positive-case counts rather than treating them as
unexplained.}

\subsubsection{Calibration}
\rmq{Per-finding Platt scaling --- one calibrator per finding, since the four are
independent --- fitted out-of-fold, with ECE per finding before and after. Explain why
thresholds were tuned on the other folds and applied to the held-out one, never tuned and
evaluated on the same data.}

\subsubsection{Final Decision}
\rmq{The retained configuration, the per-finding thresholds, and the declared limitations.}

\subsection{Cardiac Module}
\label{subsec:cardiac-module}

\subsubsection{Objective}
\rmq{Segment the left ventricle, myocardium and left atrium, and derive an ejection
fraction from the segmentation. Note the difference in task type from every other module:
this is the only pixel-level model in the system.}

\subsubsection{Dataset}
\rmq{CAMUS, with its explicit patient structure, and the external dataset used for
validation. State the benchmark from Section~\ref{subsec:sota-existing} that the in-domain
results are read against.}

\subsubsection{Model and Loss}
\rmq{U-Net with an EfficientNet-B0 encoder, and the composite loss. Explain why
cross-entropy alone is insufficient: the structures occupy a small fraction of the image, so
a model can score acceptably by predicting background everywhere. Soft Dice rewards overlap
directly and counteracts that imbalance. Also cover ignore\_index for partially labelled
masks.}

\subsubsection{Training}
\rmq{Early stopping on validation Dice with the best checkpoint retained rather than the
last, and the schedule.}

\subsubsection{In-Domain Results}
\label{subsec:cardiac-results}
\rmq{Per-structure Dice on held-out CAMUS patients, against the leaderboard figures in
Tab.~\ref{tab:camus}. Report the left atrium honestly --- it is the weakest structure here
as it is in the published results.}

\subsubsection{External Validation and the Generalisation Gap}
\rmq{The drop on the external dataset, stated plainly with both numbers. Then the
investigation, in the order it happened:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item a hypothesis that the gap was a labelling-convention mismatch, formed from a handful
        of images;
  \item connected-component analysis over the full dataset, which DISPROVED it --- report
        the reversal, it is evidence of method rather than an embarrassment;
  \item what the remaining explanation is, and what it is not.
\end{enumerate}

This is the strongest passage available in the chapter. Do not compress it.}

\subsubsection{Ejection Fraction}
\rmq{The initial bias in the derived EF, the geometric explanation --- area and volume do
not scale together --- the correction applied, and the agreement between the bias predicted
from the geometry and the bias actually observed. That agreement is the result: a correction
derived from theory and confirmed by measurement is worth more than a tuned constant. Report
MAE before and after, the correlation, balanced accuracy against the three-class chance
level, and the severe/normal confusion count.}

\subsubsection{Rejected Adaptations}
\rmq{Six strategies were tried to close the external gap and four were rejected. Report each
as hypothesis, measurement, decision --- particularly fine-tuning, which improved the
external number substantially while degrading CAMUS performance to an unacceptable degree.
The conclusion follows: the non-fine-tuned model was retained, and its domain limitation is
declared to the reasoning layer rather than hidden.}

\subsubsection{Calibration}
\rmq{Isotonic regression on the EF-derived severity classification, ECE before and after,
and the confidence ceiling that follows from it.}

\subsubsection{Final Decision}
\rmq{The retained model and the scope it declares --- specifically that it is
domain-limited, and what that forbids the reasoning layer from concluding.}

\subsection{Gallbladder Module}
\label{subsec:gallbladder-module}

\subsubsection{Objective}
\rmq{Single-label classification of gallbladder pathology at case level.}

\subsubsection{Dataset and the Mislabelled Class}
\rmq{The per-case inspection that found a class whose label did not match its images, and
the methodological lesson: the class had first been judged from a handful of frames, and
frames from one case are not a sample of a class. Sampling one frame per case across all
cases reversed the conclusion. State this as a rule, because it generalises.}

\subsubsection{Taxonomy Selection}
\rmq{The best piece of experimental economy in the project. Rather than training a model per
candidate taxonomy, the measured confusion matrix from the fine-grained model was collapsed
arithmetically to estimate the accuracy each candidate would achieve. Report the predicted
figure for the chosen taxonomy, then the measured figure after training. Their agreement is
itself a result, and it is what justified spending GPU time on one configuration instead of
several.}

\subsubsection{Training}
\rmq{Training on the fine-grained labels with class weights computed per fold from the
training portion only. Explain the marginalisation: group probabilities are SUMMED, not
obtained by mapping the argmax, because summing preserves the model's uncertainty across
members of a group.}

\subsubsection{Results}
\label{subsec:gallbladder-results}
\rmq{Balanced accuracy against the chance level for the number of classes, per-class recall,
and the case count. Report the first, much lower result before the taxonomy correction as
well --- the improvement is only meaningful with the starting point visible.}

\subsubsection{Rejected Alternatives}
\rmq{Two to report: training directly on merged labels, which was worse than merging
probabilities from fine-grained training; and USCL initialisation, which underperformed
ImageNet despite the published gain discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:sota-existing}. Give
the numbers for both.}

\subsubsection{Calibration}
\rmq{Temperature scaling, the fitted temperature, and ECE before and after.}

\subsubsection{Final Decision}
\rmq{The retained configuration and its declared limitations --- in particular whether the
module has a healthy class, since a module without one can never exclude disease.}

\subsection{Explainability Across the Imaging Modules}
\label{subsec:explainability}
\rmq{Grad-CAM applies to all three imaging modules and is therefore treated once, here.

The important methodological point: the maps were MEASURED, not inspected by eye. Report
centroid spread, area above half-maximum and normalised entropy against reference values for
uniform and concentrated attention, and the explicit test for centre bias --- a heatmap that
always points at the middle of the image regardless of content is not an explanation. State
what these statistics support (attention is concentrated) and what they cannot support (that
the attention is anatomically correct).}

\subsection{Perception Agent Output Contract}
\label{subsec:output-contract}
\rmq{The shared schema and its validation rules, each with its reason: findings are always a
list because lung is multi-label; a status of "ok" with no findings is rejected; a stated
confidence exceeding the declared ceiling is rejected. Show the schema and one worked
example from each module.

This section is the hinge between Chapters~\ref{sec:perception} and~\ref{sec:reasoning}, and
it is what makes four heterogeneous models --- a segmentation network, two classifiers and a
tree ensemble --- usable by a single reasoning layer.}
