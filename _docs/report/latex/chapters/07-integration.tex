\section{System Integration and Prototype}
\label{sec:integration}

\subsection{End-to-End Architecture}
\label{subsec:integration-arch}
\rmq{The assembled system. Refer back to Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} rather than repeating
it, and concentrate on what is added here: where each component runs, what is persisted,
and what happens when a component is unavailable.}

\subsection{Agent Communication}
\label{subsec:integration-comm}
\rmq{The shared schema as an interface contract: a single validated format written by the
perception agents and read by the reasoning agent, with atomic writes so a partially
written encounter is never read. Explain why the agents communicate through stored
encounter records rather than direct calls --- it makes each agent independently testable
and each decision reproducible after the fact.}

\subsection{Data Flow}
\label{subsec:integration-flow}
\rmq{Trace one encounter from arrival to output, naming the artefact produced at each step.
A figure or sequence diagram works better than prose here.}

\subsection{Example Clinical Scenarios}
\label{subsec:integration-scenarios}
\rmq{Three worked cases, each shown end to end: the inputs, the intermediate clinical
state, the escalation decision and the final output. These make the system concrete for a
reader who will not read the code, and they are the most persuasive part of the chapter.
Use synthetic or de-identified cases and say which.}

\subsubsection{Concordant Case}
\rmq{Triage and imaging agree, evidence is strong, key laboratory values are present.
Expected behaviour: a direct answer with a ranked differential.}

\subsubsection{Conflicting Case}
\rmq{A low triage tier alongside a high-confidence severe imaging finding. Expected
behaviour: the conflict is detected and the case is escalated rather than resolved.}

\subsubsection{Missing Data Case}
\rmq{A positive imaging finding with a key laboratory value never measured. Expected
behaviour: the absent test appears under NOT MEASURED, is barred from supporting or
contradicting any diagnosis, is named as the information that would most change the
assessment, and triggers escalation.}

\subsection{User Interface}
\label{subsec:integration-ui}
\rmq{Describe the interface actually built, at the level of fidelity it actually has. If it
is a notebook rather than an application, say so; an overstated prototype is the easiest
claim for a jury to test and disprove.}

\subsection{End-to-End Execution}
\label{subsec:integration-execution}
\rmq{Runtime and resource requirements for a complete encounter, broken down by stage, and
the practical constraints this places on bedside use.}
