\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Introduction}
\rmq{State the division explicitly in two sentences: each module was developed, measured and
decided in Chapter~\ref{sec:perception}; this chapter asks what the four results mean as a
system. A reader who has just finished Chapter 5 needs to know they are not about to read
the same numbers again.}

\subsection{The Modules Side by Side}
\label{subsec:results-comparison}
\rmq{One table drawing together all four modules: task type, model family, headline metric,
the baseline it is read against, case count, calibration method and ECE after calibration.
Metrics differ across modules and cannot be compared directly --- say so --- but the
distance of each from its own baseline can be.

The observation worth drawing out: the modules differ enormously in headline metric while
being comparable in how far each sits above its own chance level. That is the honest way to
read four numbers on four different tasks.}

\subsection{Calibration as a System Property}
\label{subsec:results-calibration}
\rmq{Four modules, four calibration methods --- isotonic, Platt per finding, temperature,
Platt --- chosen per module rather than uniformly. Explain why the choice differs and what
each suited.

Then the system-level argument, which is the real content of this section: calibration is
what makes four heterogeneous models comparable to one another. Without it the reasoning
layer would be combining numbers on incommensurable scales, and the escalation thresholds in
Section~\ref{subsec:reasoning-escalation} would have no defensible basis. Calibration is not
a finishing touch here; it is the precondition for the architecture working at all.}

\subsection{What the Explainability Results Do and Do Not Establish}
\label{subsec:results-explainability}
\rmq{Summarise the Grad-CAM statistics across modules without repeating the tables from
Section~\ref{subsec:explainability}. Be precise about the limit: concentrated attention is
evidence that the model is responding to localised image content rather than to global
brightness or position. It is NOT evidence that the attention falls on the anatomically
correct structure, which would require expert annotation this project does not have. Stating
that boundary is worth more than the statistics themselves.}

\subsection{Clinical Reasoning Evaluation}
\label{subsec:results-reasoning}
\rmq{The benchmark cases and what was checked: schema validity, whether the hallucination
guard fired when it should, and whether the escalation decision matched the intended one.
Report the small size of the benchmark in the same breath as the results, not in a later
limitations section.}

\subsection{Overall System Evaluation}
\label{subsec:results-system}
\rmq{The properties that only appear once the modules are assembled, and that no per-module
metric can show: graceful degradation when a module is unavailable, correct propagation of a
module's declared limits into the escalation decision, and the behaviour on the worked
scenarios of Section~\ref{subsec:integration-scenarios}. This is the section that justifies
the architecture rather than the models.}

\subsection{Rejected Experiments}
\label{subsec:results-rejected}
\rmq{Each rejection is reported inside the module that prompted it in
Chapter~\ref{sec:perception}. Collect them here in a single table --- hypothesis,
measurement, decision --- so that the pattern is visible: external-dataset integration,
the augmentation upgrade that changed nothing measurable, USCL initialisation, cardiac
fine-tuning, the alternative gallbladder taxonomy, the aggressive triage class weighting,
and the coarse reason-for-visit grouping.

Then the point the table makes, which no individual entry makes on its own: seven
well-motivated changes were tested and rejected on measurement. That is the methodological
rule of Section~\ref{subsec:methodology} shown working, and it is the strongest available
evidence that the retained results were not selected for being favourable.}

\subsection{Strengths}
\label{subsec:results-strengths}
\rmq{Argue from measurements, not from intent. The defensible claims: case-level evaluation
throughout, on three datasets that did not provide it; calibrated confidence carried end to
end rather than discarded at the module boundary; explicit representation of absent evidence;
and safety decisions that do not depend on a language model. Each of these is checkable by an
examiner, which is why they are the ones to claim.}

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:results-limitations}
\rmq{State each as a bound on a specific claim rather than as a generic disclaimer. Small
case-level datasets; public collections rather than consecutive clinical series; no external
validation for most modules; the lung case mapping is a filename heuristic with residual
uncertainty; the cardiac module is domain-limited; no clinical outcome validation; no
prospective evaluation; the reasoning benchmark is small.

For each, name what it forbids concluding. A limitation that does not change what may be
claimed is not worth listing.}

\subsection{Clinical Interpretation}
\label{subsec:results-discussion}
\rmq{What the numbers mean for a clinician rather than for a leaderboard. Which modules are
close to being useful and which are not, and the argument that a well-calibrated model of
moderate accuracy can be more useful in this system than a more accurate model whose
confidence cannot be trusted --- because the reasoning layer can act on the first and cannot
safely act on the second.

Close by returning to the four difficulties of Section~\ref{subsec:problem} and assessing
each honestly: which have been addressed, which have been partially addressed, and which
remain open. Resist the temptation to mark all four as solved; the fourth in particular is
mitigated rather than solved.}
