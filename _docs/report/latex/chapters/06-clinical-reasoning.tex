\section{Clinical Reasoning Agent}
\label{sec:reasoning}

\subsection{Motivation}
\label{subsec:reasoning-motivation}
\rmq{Why a separate reasoning layer rather than a larger perception model: the perception
modules answer questions about images, and no combination of them answers a question about
a patient. Restate the two separations from Section~\ref{subsec:architecture} briefly and
move on.}

\subsection{Clinical State Representation}
\label{subsec:clinical-state}

\begin{keystatement}
Absent is not normal. A test that was never performed cannot support or contradict any
diagnosis, and a model with no healthy class can never exclude disease.
\end{keystatement}

\rmq{The structured state: findings with confidence and evidence grade, vitals and labs
with reference ranges and high/low flags, and an explicit NOT MEASURED block. Give the
rendered form that is passed to the model, since it makes the design concrete in a way a
schema listing does not.}

\subsection{Integration of Triage and Ultrasound}
\label{subsec:reasoning-integration}
\rmq{How the two perception outputs are normalised into one representation despite
different shapes (a single tier with confidence versus a list of findings), and how
evidence grade is assigned: experimental when the confidence is uncalibrated, limited when
evidence is thin or confidence is low, strong otherwise.}

\subsection{Missing Data Handling}
\label{subsec:reasoning-missing}
\rmq{The distinction between measured-and-normal, measured-and-abnormal, and not-measured,
and why the third must be represented rather than defaulted. Give the list of key
laboratory values whose absence alone is enough to change the escalation decision.}

\subsection{Conflict Detection}
\label{subsec:reasoning-conflict}
\rmq{Detection of disagreement between agents --- for example a low triage tier alongside a
high-confidence severe imaging finding. Conflicts are surfaced, not resolved: the system
does not have the information to decide which source is wrong, so it escalates instead.}

\subsection{Medical Knowledge Base}
\label{subsec:reasoning-kb}
\rmq{Corpus selection, licensing and scope. Be explicit about what the corpus does NOT
cover, since retrieval that silently returns nothing relevant is worse than no retrieval.}

\subsection{Retrieval-Augmented Generation}
\label{subsec:reasoning-rag}
\rmq{Chunking, embedding and retrieval strategy, and the citation requirement in the
prompt. Note the deliberate behaviour when retrieval is unavailable: the prompt states that
no evidence was retrieved, rather than omitting the fact, so the model cannot imply
guideline support it never had.}

\subsection{Medical LLM}
\label{subsec:reasoning-llm}
\rmq{Model choice and local deployment, and the reason for running locally rather than
through an API: patient data does not leave the machine. Report inference cost honestly.}

\subsection{Clinical Reasoning Pipeline}
\label{subsec:reasoning-pipeline}
\rmq{The full sequence: clinical state, escalation decision computed BEFORE the model runs,
retrieval, prompt construction, generation, structured parsing, validation, and the final
output. A figure is worth including here.}

\subsection{Differential Diagnosis}
\label{subsec:reasoning-differential}
\rmq{Output format: ranked diagnoses with likelihood, supporting and contradicting evidence
drawn only from the state, the information that would most change the assessment, and a
recommended next step. Note that the system recommends next steps and does not prescribe
treatment.}

\subsection{Confidence and Uncertainty}
\label{subsec:reasoning-confidence}
\rmq{How model confidence, evidence grade and overall case quality combine, and the grading
of the case as a whole (good / moderate / poor) with the named reasons that produced the
grade. Conflicts weigh more heavily than any single weak finding.}

\subsection{Escalation Policy}
\label{subsec:reasoning-escalation}
\rmq{The deterministic triggers, each computed from the structured state: disagreement
between agents, poor case quality, a high-risk finding on non-strong evidence, a high-risk
finding below decisive confidence, positive imaging with a key laboratory value absent, and
no positive finding where the models cannot exclude disease. Present these as a table.
Emphasise that not one of them depends on the language model having noticed anything.}

\subsection{Safety and Limitations}
\label{subsec:reasoning-safety}
\rmq{The hallucination guard: every term the model cites is checked against the vocabulary
of the state, and any citation of a NOT MEASURED value is an error. A differential citing
tests that were never run is withheld entirely rather than displayed with a warning beside
it --- justify that choice. Then state the limitations plainly: the reasoning layer has not
been validated against clinical outcomes, and the benchmark used is small.}
